Featured Case Study

AI-Enabled Document Review — Reading the documents nobody wants to read

Applied AI

A production pipeline that reviews insurance servicing documentation — new business, cancellations, endorsements, and payments — against carrier rules, and escalates to a human wherever it isn't confident.

Problem

Underwriting risk hides in documents. Servicing paperwork arrives in no consistent schema, and every carrier applies its own rules to it, so the only way to catch a discrepancy was for a person to read every page against the right rulebook. That does not scale, and worse, it fails unevenly: attention drops on page forty in a way it doesn't on page one. The cost of a missed detail in insurance is not a bad user experience, it's a mispriced policy.

Architecture

Scroll the diagram sideways to see the full pipeline →

1
Intake. Servicing documents arrive as PDFs or images, in whatever condition the sender produced them.
2
Multimodal analysis. A vision-capable model reads the pages directly rather than working from an OCR transcript — on a poor scan, OCR produces confident garbage, which is worse than no answer.
3
Carrier-rule context. The relevant carrier's rules are supplied alongside the document, so the model is checking against the rulebook that actually governs this policy instead of a general notion of correctness.
4
Structured checklist. Output is a JSON checklist of 50+ discrete items — not prose. Every finding is addressable, comparable, and checkable by something other than a human reading it.
5
Business-rule evaluation. Internal deterministic rules evaluate the checklist. Rules, not the model, have the final say on anything that must be exact.
6
Confidence and status. Each transaction resolves to a status with a confidence attached, which is what makes the next step a decision rather than a guess.
7
Targeted human review. Low-confidence results route to a reviewer. A randomized sample is reviewed regardless of confidence, so the pipeline is measured on work it thought it got right.
Governance
  • Confidence-gated automation — the pipeline is allowed to decline. Low-confidence results route to a person rather than being passed through with a caveat, and a randomized sample is reviewed even when confidence is high.
  • Data residency by design — documents carrying PII are processed on locally hosted models. Model choice follows the data classification, not the other way round.
Evaluation

Evaluated against approximately 1,000 real applications before production rollout, with human reviewers comparing AI-generated findings against source documents and established review criteria. In production, evaluation continues through targeted review of low-confidence results plus randomized quality-control sampling.

Results

Processes approximately 15,000 insurance servicing documents per month across new business, cancellations, endorsements, and payments. Approximately 85% are processed automatically, with human review concentrated on low-confidence results and randomized quality-control samples.

Typical processing time decreased from approximately 7 minutes to 2 minutes per transaction, blended across both the automated path and the work that escalates to a human.

The dedicated review team went from 10 reviewers to 5. That is review capacity redeployed, not payroll removed — the other five moved into other departments.

Stack

Claude and OpenAI APIs, AWS Bedrock and SageMaker, and locally hosted models for PII-bearing documents. AWS Textract handles structured extraction on payment transactions, where the incoming documents are standardized enough that deterministic extraction is the right tool. Python and SQL Server, on AWS and Azure.

Case Study

iBinderbook — One system of record for an insurance agency

Platform / CRM

A custom agency management and CRM platform integrating policy workflows, lead tracking, and automation — designed to increase throughput and reduce manual friction.

Problem

Manual workflows and fragmented systems slowed agent velocity and introduced operational risk across policy management, follow-ups, and commission logic.

Approach
  • Designed end-to-end workflow orchestration across sales → policy → servicing
  • Automated repeatable tasks with clear ownership and auditability
  • Integrated operational reporting for visibility into throughput and bottlenecks
Scale

650+ users on a continuously evolving operational platform spanning CRM, policies, accounting, reporting, dashboards, metrics, analytics, and integrated communications. In production since 2014 and still the system the business runs on.

Results

Commercial agency management systems license per seat, per month. At 650 users that line runs well into six figures a year — gross, before netting what the platform costs to build and run in-house. Exact per-seat rates move over time, so the magnitude is the honest claim, not a quoted figure.

The licensing figure is the easy half of the comparison. The harder half is that owning the product means the policy, commission, and servicing workflows match how this agency actually operates, rather than how a vendor assumes it does — which is the reason the build decision held for twelve years, not the line item.

Architecture

Proprietary enterprise application architecture integrating operational workflows, CRM capabilities, policy management, accounting functions, reporting and analytics, and API-driven communications. Integrated communications run on Twilio — voice, SMS, automated messaging, IVRs, and number management.
